# Portable whiteboard five-bar: drive feasibility
**Research checkpoint: 2026-11-02 — paused for later review.**

This preserves the prior mechanical analysis, assumptions and unresolved choices. It is **not a released mechanical design, a purchase list or a hardware operating envelope**. The accepted kinematic preview remains unchanged at baseline `15b9456f5068b92956375eb7503a46d8e0d0cce4`. No drive architecture, pulley width, CAD assembly or physical test has been finalized.

## Decision snapshot

- Direct drive with the reference acrylic links is not supported by the static gravity results: even nominal demand exceeds the motors' **0.30 N·m holding** rating.
- A preliminary alternative is **20 × 10 × 1 mm aluminum rectangular tube**, with approximately **20:1 reduction per shoulder**. This is a candidate for testing, not a validated selection.
- A two-stage **matched-profile 2 mm-pitch timing-belt** drive, 20:80 then 20:100 teeth, is worth revisiting. Large printed pulleys and small metal pulleys are a prototype concept; actual belt ratings, support bearings and packaging remain open.
- The largest unresolved items are running torque at the actual electrical settings, dynamic loads, stiffness, transmission capacity, ledge stability and mechanically supported docking/carry retention.
- Resume with one supported, loaded shoulder test and a measured operating envelope before committing to both complete arms.

## Static gravity model

Use the actual closed-loop five-bar, uniform link masses at their midpoints, elbow point masses and a point tool mass. Let:

- mp = mass of each proximal link; md = mass of each distal link;
- me = added mass at each elbow; mt = total tool mass;
- C=(mp+md)/2+me; D=md+mt; g=9.80665 m/s²;
- J = tool-position Jacobian with respect to the two motor angles.

Using **metres, kilograms and radians**:

```text
U = g [ C (yL + yR) + D yT ]
tau_i = dU/dtheta_i = g [ C p cos(theta_i) + D J_yi ]
```

These are balancing torques; gravity's generalized torque has the opposite sign. This includes closed-chain load sharing, unlike assigning the complete weight times reach to each motor. A prior five-pose analytic versus centered finite-difference potential-energy check agreed within 3.61e-10 N·m.

Excluded: acceleration/inertia, pen drag and pressure, friction, cable loads, transmission losses (except the explicitly assumed efficiency below), backlash, elastic deflection, joint stresses, thermal limits and measured motor torque-speed behavior. Fixed motors are excluded from moving gravitational energy but still count toward ledge balance.

“Demo peak” means the larger absolute motor torque across the prior guarded approach/drawing/return path, excluding the supported singular dock. Grid peaks are **sampled maxima, not continuous global bounds**; grid statistics cover guarded cells without an additional connectivity filter.

## Acrylic: direct-drive screen

Assumed PMMA density 1190 kg/m³ and short-term elastic modulus 3300 MPa, consistent with standard PLEXIGLAS GS/XT data at stated test conditions. [Material data, p.4][pmma]

Reference links: 20 mm wide × 3 mm thick; each elbow adds 5 g; tool adds 25 g.

Even the extreme light case reached approximately 0.455 N·m on the guarded grid, before dynamics, with stiffness unvalidated. Ideal lossless reductions against **holding** torque for the reference case would be 2.38:1 nominal, 3.38:1 demo and 5.21:1 refined grid: these are arithmetic lower bounds, **not recommended drive ratios**.

At the 70 mm staging tip, the reference case needs approximately ±0.830 N·m. The unsupported symmetric near-flat limit is also approximately ±0.830 N·m; the exactly flat singular rest does not have a regular Jacobian derivative. Support at the final pose alone does not validate the approach.

Thin flat acrylic is also weak out of plane. The isolated cantilever screen uses I=bt³/12 and delta/F=L³/(3EI); very large thin-strip predictions violate small-deflection assumptions and are **not actual assembled-linkage deflections**. Thickness trades stiffness for substantial added gravity load. Brittle joints, hole preparation and local reinforcement need attention. [PLEXIGLAS drilling guidance][drilling]

## Aluminum and reduction comparison

Preliminary preferred section: **20 × 10 × 1 mm rectangular tube**, with 20 mm in the drawing plane and 10 mm normal to the board. Use nominal aluminum density 2700 kg/m³ and E≈69 GPa. Actual alloy, temper, wall thickness, holes, joints and tube tolerances remain to be selected. [6061 tube data][al-density] · [Modulus data][al-modulus]

This section gives approximately 390 g of bare links. Relative to 20 × 2 mm aluminum flat bar, it is about 40% heavier but has approximately 2.08× in-plane and 67× out-of-plane second moment of area. Compared with 20 × 3 mm flat bar it is about 7% lighter with approximately 1.39× in-plane and 20× out-of-plane stiffness for the same material. An isolated 855 mm tube cantilever under 1 N normal force gives approximately 3.36 mm deflection; **not a prediction for the complete closed-chain assembly**.

Assume **15 g at each elbow and 50 g total tool mass**:

| Aluminum section, mm | Moving mass, g | Nominal each, N·m | Demo peak, N·m | Guarded 2 mm grid peak, N·m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Flat 20 × 2 | 359 | 1.208 | 1.722 | 2.664 |
| Flat 20 × 3 | 498 | 1.606 | 2.258 | 3.429 |
| **Tube 20 × 10 × 1** | **470** | **1.526** | **2.151** | **3.276** |
| Tube 20 × 10 × 1.5 | 644 | 2.024 | 2.821 | 4.233 |
| Tube 20 × 20 × 1 | 609 | 1.924 | 2.687 | 4.042 |

With 10 g elbows and 40 g tool, the preferred tube case becomes approximately 450 g, 1.429 N·m nominal, 2.004 N·m demo and 3.033 N·m grid peak.

For the heavier preferred-tube case, motor torque = output torque / (ratio × efficiency). **80% total drivetrain efficiency is an assumption, not measured or guaranteed.**

| Reduction | Motor gravity torque at demo peak | Motor gravity torque at grid peak |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10:1 | 0.269 N·m | 0.410 N·m |
| 15:1 | 0.179 N·m | 0.273 N·m |
| **20:1** | **0.134 N·m** | **0.205 N·m** |

20:1 is therefore a useful prototype candidate, but not proof of adequate full-workspace running torque. A 2× allowance over the grid gravity demand would require approximately 0.410 N·m at the motor even at 20:1 and assumed efficiency—above its holding rating.

At a 40 mm/s pen speed and 20:1 ratio, prior kinematic estimates gave approximately 9.57 motor rpm for the nominal pose's worst direction, 18.3 rpm maximum on the actual demo path, and 112 rpm for the worst direction over the guarded map. Values halve at 20 mm/s. A slow central drawing window is the first qualification target, not an assumption that all reachable points work equally well.

## Timing-belt concept: not yet sized

“GT2” alone is insufficient procurement information: confirm **2 mm pitch and the exact mutually compatible belt/pulley tooth profile**, belt construction and manufacturer ratings.

Per shoulder:

```text
motor/input shaft: 20T -> 80T intermediate pulley   = 4:1
same intermediate shaft: 20T -> 100T output       = 5:1
total reduction                                  = 20:1
```

Two separate loops per shoulder would be needed for this layout. Center distances, belt lengths, width, tooth wrap, tension adjustment, flanges and depth stacking are not designed.

For 2 mm pitch, pitch diameter = teeth × pitch / pi:

| Teeth | Pitch diameter, mm |
|---|---:|
| 20 | 12.732 |
| 80 | 50.930 |
| 100 | 63.662 |

At the 100T output's 31.831 mm pitch radius, tight-minus-slack tension **difference** is approximately 67.5 N for the 2.151 N·m demo load and 103 N for the 3.276 N·m grid load. These are **not** total belt tension, pretension or bearing reaction; dynamics and margin are absent.

**No 6, 9 or 15 mm belt width is selected.** Larger-pitch Gates power ratings cannot certify a generic 2 mm-pitch belt. Use the exact belt maker's applicable tables and tooth-engagement rules; inspect the actual small-pulley wrap. The cited Gates manuals provide design principles, not a blanket rating for this proposed printed drive. [Gates drive-design manual][gatesmanual] · [PowerGrip belt families][gatesbelts]

Prototype construction direction:
- Buy small metal pulleys; consider printing large pulley bodies and guards. Printed tooth accuracy, warping, hub slip, wear, creep and strength require testing.
- Use secure metal hubs/shafts and bearing-supported intermediate/output shafts. Add tube-end plugs/crush sleeves so fasteners do not collapse thin walls.
- Do not suspend long arms directly on stepper shafts. Even the input 20T pulley at 0.134 N·m implies about 21 N tension difference before pretension—already near the motor datasheet's radial-load figure. Independently supported input shaft/coupling or a verified bearing-load layout may be needed.
- A 100T output pulley suggests a raised base pivot around **40 mm or more** as an initial packaging estimate, not a checked CAD dimension. **The preview still uses 10 mm.** Any raise requires updated geometry, park clearance and reachable-area checks before manufacture.
- Belts may reduce gear-tooth backlash but are not zero-compliance or zero-lost-motion transmissions.
- Provide an independent mechanical support/catch/restraint and carry lock. Neither motor holding current nor belt integrity alone is a safe power-off support.

Earlier spur-gear alternative: 20:80 then 20:100 teeth also gives 20:1. A module-1 100T spur gear has approximately 102 mm outside diameter and suggested a 55–60 mm-or-higher base pivot for packaging; tooth strength and bearings were not designed. Worm self-locking must not be treated as a dependable safety brake. [KHK spur gears][spur] · [KHK worm gears][worm]

## Resume here

1. **Confirm electrical setup:** PSU voltage, TMC2209 carrier, actual RMS/peak current convention, cooling and running mode. Obtain or measure a useful torque-speed curve for this exact motor/setup.
2. **Freeze a modest first envelope:** drawing window, feed, acceleration and desired drawing accuracy; include pen pressure/drag and cable routing. Recreate the gravity calculation from the equations above and preserve scripts/results alongside the next dated note.
3. **Weigh the proposed assembly:** real tool/pen-lift mass, elbows, fasteners, bearings, tube end plugs and moving wiring. Recompute torque and include inertia, losses and reserve.
4. **Select and rate a real transmission:** matching belt profile/pitch, manufacturer, width, wrap, lengths, pretension, shaft spacing and bearing reactions; or a separately rated gear design. No purchasing decision has been made.
5. **Make a CAD packaging/structure pass:** depth layers, real section envelopes, crush sleeves, bearing support, pivot height, mechanical stops, supported docking and carry latch. Check full swept volumes and pinch points, not only centerlines.
6. **Check the ledge:** complete center of mass, contact reactions, tipping under pen force/acceleration, restraint and supported power-off behavior within the 45.72 mm support footprint.
7. **Test one supported loaded shoulder first:** measure missed steps, heat, deflection, backlash, creep and drive loads with guarding/restraint. Only then qualify the complete five-bar and its low-speed central drawing window.
8. **Integrate control later:** a dedicated nonlinear five-bar adapter, gearing, homing, direction, acceleration/step timing, limits, pen lift and fault handling are required. Current geometry exports are not executable commands.

No app changes, firmware, hardware connection, purchases, CAD fabrication release or physical validation are part of this checkpoint.
